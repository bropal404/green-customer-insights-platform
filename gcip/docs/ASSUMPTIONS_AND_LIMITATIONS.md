# Assumptions and limitations

Every number the platform uses is either a sourced value or a labelled assumption. This page lists them all, stage
by stage, with where each is set, followed by the limits of each stage. Values in the tables are read from
`configs/*.yaml`; results come from the 30,000-customer run in `demo/`. To change an assumption, edit the config and
rerun (`./run.sh --quick` or `--rebuild`): nothing is hard-coded in the code.

## 1. Scope and data

**Assumptions**

- **A synthetic bank.**
  - 30,000 customers, England and Wales, each the main account holder of one household.
  - Generated by a causal simulator calibrated on public UK data: Census 2021, DESNZ NEED homes, DVSA MOT cars,
    Ofgem prices, DESNZ factors and the National Travel Survey.
  - Every simulator parameter is in `configs/simulation.yaml`, with its source or the word "assumption"; the table
    below lists the assumptions.
- **Take-up.** Take-up follows a documented logistic model (`gcip.simulate.labels`): each customer's true drivers
  (what they drive, how they heat their home, their income) plus hidden traits that the platform never sees.
  - The take-up rate among eligible customers is set per product: EV Finance 5.5%, Green Home Loan 4.5%, Solar Finance 3.5%, Paperless Banking 12%, Green Investments 6%, Green Loans 3.5% over six months.
- **Two dates.**
  - Training snapshot 2026-03-31: features use only data up to this date.
  - Labels: take-ups from 1 April to 30 September 2026.
  - Live snapshot 2026-09-30: what the dashboards show.
  - 12 months of history behind each snapshot.

**Limitations**

- **Metrics describe this simulated world, not real Lloyds customers.** They show the method works when the signal is
  in the data. They are not comparable with results on other datasets, as the brief itself notes.
- **Hidden traits cap what any model can do.** The simulator's exact probability (the "oracle") reaches a test AUC of
  0.79-0.91; our models reach 0.63-0.82.
- **Being shown a suggestion has no effect on take-up in the simulator.** So the measured uplift is close to zero
  (+0.7 points, 95% interval -5.7 to +7.1). The system
  measures uplift correctly; this world simply has none.
- **Geography and coverage.**
  - England and Wales only, because Census 2021 covers only these.
  - Area context is at local-authority level, not postcode.
  - MOT data is national and covers only cars aged 3 or more.
  - Energy prices are GB averages, so regional price differences are not modelled.
- **What the bank cannot see.**
  - Joint accounts appear only as bills paid from elsewhere.
  - Spending through other banks is invisible: about 14% of households pay energy bills from another bank.
- **Gaps carried over from the data card** (`docs/DATA_CARD.md`, "Limitations"):
  - Insurers selling both motor and home cover write identical statement text for both.
  - Dual-fuel and single-fuel bills can read the same.
  - Heating oil, LPG, public charging and water prices are assumptions.
- **Public data has fixed dates** (see `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`): Census 2021, NEED 2025, MOT 2024/25, DESNZ factors
  2026, Ofgem caps to Q4 2026.

## 2. Source systems and FR1 preprocessing

**Assumptions**

- **Seven source systems, each documented.** Formats, keys, units, date formats, code tables and known problems are
  in `configs/source_systems.yaml`.
- **The control file is trusted.** Every extract must match its row count and SHA-256 hash before it is parsed;
  otherwise the load stops.
- **Problems the simulator injects, and how often:** card replay 0.2%; digital orphan 0.3%; crm duplicate contact 1%; crm prospect 1%; legacy account format 1%; region upper case 5%; region lower case 3%.
- **How each problem is handled:**
  - Exact replayed card rows are duplicates and are removed.
  - Prospects with no customer reference, and analytics IDs that match no customer, are quarantined with a reason,
    never guessed.
  - A customer with several CRM contact IDs is merged into one.
  - Region names are re-cased to the ONS spelling.
  - Legacy account numbers are normalised.
- **Point-in-time Customer 360.** Customer 360 joins only records dated on or before the snapshot. It is built in
  SQLite, and transactions are streamed whole customers at a time.

What the load did on the 30,000 build:
- 7 source systems, 71 files; 71 passed size and SHA-256 checks; row counts all match.
- Transactions: 20,691,226 card-feed rows (41,301 replayed duplicates removed) and 7,616,570 payments-hub rows (75,989 legacy account numbers normalised) -> 28,266,495 transactions in 30 parts.
- Quarantined from campaign_responses: 451 (prospect: no customer reference).
- Quarantined from digital_activity: 1,890 (uid matches no customer).
- Audit: 8 of 8 tables identical to the bank's system of record.
- Audit: 28,266,495 of 28,266,495 transactions recovered; 0 missing, 0 unexpected, 0 on the wrong account, 0 on the wrong customer, 0 with any field different.

**Limitations**

- **The loader handles the problems we know about.** Real feeds also have late-arriving and restated data, cut-off
  and time-zone effects, foreign currency, and reversal and chargeback chains; none of these is simulated.
- **The audit relies on the simulator.** It compares every table and transaction with what the simulator's
  systems held before extraction (the truth vault). A real bank has no such copy, and would reconcile against
  control totals from each system of record instead.
- **All amounts are in GBP.** There is no foreign-currency handling.

## 3. FR2 categoriser

**Assumptions**

- **Taxonomy.** Eight categories with sub-categories (`configs/taxonomy.yaml`). Each transaction gets exactly one
  category, which is the roll-up of its predicted sub-category.
- **Layer order.**
  1. Rules, which fire only when near-certain.
  2. A merchant lookup: a text seen in training at least 3 times with at least
     95% purity.
  3. A TF-IDF model: character n-grams [2, 5] and word n-grams
     [1, 2], logistic C = 6.0.
  - Embeddings, FinBERT and DistilBERT are optional and switched off (validation gave them zero weight).
- **Generalisation test.** 20% of merchants are held out of training entirely.
- **Boundary rules**, where two categories meet:
- **B1** Fuel vs Automotive: energy that moves the vehicle is Fuel; anything that keeps a vehicle owned, financed, insured, taxed, maintained or parked is Automotive.
- **B2** Public EV charging is Fuel (transport energy). Home EV charging is part of the household Electricity bill and is not separable.
- **B3** Shop purchases at a fuel forecourt are Other (forecourt_shop). Forecourt terminals report the same merchant and MCC for fuel and shop, so the model separates them using the amount.
- **B4** Electricity or Gas only when the payment is for a single-fuel supply. One payment covering both fuels (dual fuel) is Utilities, as are water and delivered heating oil or LPG.
- **B5** Prepayment top-ups take the category of the meter they top up (Electricity or Gas). Generic top-up networks without a fuel tag are still labelled by the meter.
- **B6** Congestion, ULEZ, clean-air-zone and toll charges are Automotive even when paid to a transport authority; fares paid to the same authority are Public Transport.
- **B7** Taxis and ride-hailing are Other: they are private hire, not shared or scheduled transport. Bike and e-scooter share schemes are Public Transport.
- **B8** Large appliance retailers are Home Improvement (white goods change home energy use); furniture and homeware are Other.
- **B9** Motor insurance and breakdown cover are Automotive; home and life insurance are Other.
- **B10** Refunds and credits take the category of the merchant that paid them (an energy supplier refund stays in its energy category). Income credits are Other.
- **B11** Mortgage and rent payments are Other (housing) but are used as evidence of tenure.

**Limitations**

- **New merchants are harder.** Accuracy on merchants never seen in training is 90.4%, against
  99.0% for known ones.
- **The weakest boundary is Electricity/Gas vs Utilities** when a bill names no fuel (rule B4): 12-17% of
  transactions cross it wrongly. Forecourt shop purchases vs fuel cross wrongly about 12% of the time for unseen
  merchants.
- **Home EV charging is invisible.** It sits inside the household electricity bill and can't be separated (rule B2).
- **The descriptions are synthetic.** They come from a generated lexicon. On 68,000 real US bank descriptions, coarse
  agreement is 82%. Real UK narratives may be
  messier than ours.

## 4. Features and labels

**Assumptions**

- **Fuel litres** = spend divided by that week's DESNZ pump price.
- **Energy kWh** = (bill minus standing charge) divided by that quarter's Ofgem cap unit rate.
- **Dual-fuel bills** are split using Ofgem's typical consumption: electricity 2500, gas 9500 kWh.
- **Unknown car fuel** is taken as petrol. Without fuel evidence, mileage is the
  NTS average.
- **Coverage flags** record how much of each behaviour is visible, and they feed confidence.
- **No leakage.** Features use only data up to the snapshot, and only *predicted* categories, never the truth.
- **Labels.** A take-up is a product opened in the window, according to the bank's product system. For a customer
  eligible at the snapshot, this reproduces the simulator's labels exactly (247 of 247 on the 2,000-customer build).

**Limitations**

- **Energy inputs recovered from spending are rough.**
  - Gas is within 20% of the truth for 39% of customers, electricity for
    39%. Median errors are 28%
    and 30%.
  - Fuel is much better: 83% within 20%, median error
    7.4%.
- **Fixed after the shipped run: energy scaled per fuel.** Gas and electricity were scaled to a year using one shared month count (the larger of the two), so a gap in one fuel's bills was not scaled up. That affected about 10% of gas and 4% of electricity estimates (customers whose two fuels show different months; measured on four parts of the 30,000 build). The code now scales each supply by its own months (`scripts/run_scenario_tests.py` checks it). The shipped 30,000-customer results in `demo/` predate the fix; `./run.sh --quick` and `--rebuild` use it.
- **National average prices.** Real pump and tariff prices vary by place and supplier.
- **Prepayment, other-bank bills and cash spending** leave little or no evidence. The confidence score and the PCAF
  data-quality score say so.

## 5. FR3 propensity

**Assumptions**

- **Inputs.** One model per product, each using a curated list of 21-40 inputs. Age and income band may inform the
  score but are never shown as a reason.
- **Training.** LightGBM with 40 random-search trials (chosen on validation by
  average precision), 5 bootstrap models, and out-of-fold isotonic calibration
  bounded to 0.001-0.95.
- **Confidence** = sqrt(data coverage) x 1 / (1 + coefficient of variation across the bootstrap models).
- **Threshold.** A suggestion needs a propensity of at least 2.0 x the product's take-up rate among
  eligible training customers, and confidence of at least 0.45.
- **Reason codes.** TreeSHAP contributions summed per reason code. A code is shown only when the customer's data
  supports it.

**Limitations**

- **The model predicts who is likely to take a product, not who changes because of the suggestion.** It may favour
  people who would have taken it anyway. The 10% hold-out control group is how the bank would measure the real
  effect.
- **Few take-ups for some products** (validation / test): Green Home Loan 49 / 31, Solar Finance 31 / 56, Green Investments 45 / 60. Their metrics have wide uncertainty, though
  validation and test agree. Mean ROC-AUC is 0.720 on validation and
  0.727 on test.
- **The calibration bound caps propensity at 95%.** The thresholds are policy choices, not optimised for profit.
- **Selection rates differ by income band** for EV and solar finance, because take-up differs
  (`docs/RESPONSIBLE_AI.md`).

## 6. FR4 carbon and money

**Assumptions**

- **Boundary.** well-to-wheel: combustion or generation, plus upstream fuel production, plus
  grid losses.
- **Factors.** DESNZ GHG conversion factors for company reporting 2026, flat file v1.2.
- **Framing.** Avoided emissions for the customer (GHG Protocol and WBCSD guidance); PCAF financed emissions and
  data-quality scores for the bank.
- **Horizons**, in years: GP01 4, GP02 15, GP03 25, GP04 1, GP05 1, GP06 5.
- **The grid factor is held flat** over the horizon. That is conservative for EVs and heat pumps, and generous for
  solar.
- **Green Investments footprints** are built from ONS 2024 greenhouse-gas intensity by industry, using assumed sector
  weights for a broad UK equity fund and a sustainability-focused fund. 25%
  of holdings is assumed to switch.
- **Sensitivity.** Each key input is moved by ±10-20% one at a time (`metrics/impact_sensitivity.csv`, the tornado
  chart): pump_price 0.2, km_per_litre 0.2, heat_pump_efficiency 0.2, boiler_efficiency 0.1, pv_yield 0.2, dual_fuel_split 0.2.

**Limitations**

- **Manufacturing emissions are not counted.** The well-to-wheel boundary leaves out the emissions of making an EV
  battery, solar panels or a heat pump. Lifetime savings would be lower on a full life-cycle basis.
- **The comparison is with keeping what they have.** Avoided emissions assume the customer would otherwise keep their
  current car or heating. Someone who would have switched anyway avoids less.
- **Prices are held at the snapshot date** for running-cost savings. The live snapshot uses prices in force on
  2026-09-30.
- **Investment footprints are proxies** (PCAF data quality 5), with no holdings data. No money figure is given for
  investments, because returns are uncertain and the bank gives information, not advice.
- **Paperless savings are tiny per customer**, so Paperless is exempt from the minimum-CO2e rule.
- **When inputs are missing, no number is given.** The suggestion is withheld as "impact cannot be estimated".

## 7. Suggestions (FR5-FR7)

**Assumptions** (`configs/pipeline.yaml` `recommend:`, `configs/products.yaml`)

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | the bank's product rules in `configs/products.yaml` (blocks, human-review flags) |
| Marketing consent | required |
| Impact required | True |
| Minimum lift over the take-up rate | 2.0x |
| Minimum confidence | 0.45 |
| Minimum CO2e avoided per year | 100 kg (exempt: GP04) |

| Priority | propensity x confidence x memory weight |

**Limitations**

- **Thresholds are business choices, set and visible in config.** They have not been tuned against a real profit or
  complaint target.
- **The priority score multiplies three quantities on different scales.** It orders suggestions; it is not a
  probability of anything.
- **The routing rules are hand-written.** Green Loans gives way to a more specific product when one applies.

## 8. Customer memory

**Assumptions** (`configs/pipeline.yaml` `memory:`)

- **Events.** Every CRM campaign contact and every response in the apps is a memory event. The last
  365 days count.
- **Response weights** multiply priority: clicked 1.5, viewed 0.85, no_response 0.7, dismissed 0.5, not_interested 0.2. The product of weights is bounded to
  0.05, 2.
- **Fatigue.** 3 contacts in a row with no engagement pause the product for
  180 days.
- **Cool-downs** after "dismissed" and "not interested": dismissed 90, not_interested 180 days. "I already have this"
  stops the product.
- **Memory never changes propensity, eligibility or impact** (the brief's guardrail).

**Limitations**

- **The weights are set by hand, not learned.** There is no data on how a response predicts later interest, because
  the simulator gives contacts no effect.
- **The memory replay simulates responses.** It shows the mechanism (`metrics/memory_replay.json`), not a measured
  behaviour change.
- **No free-text feedback is captured.**

## 9. Explanations and the LLM

**Assumptions**

- **The template is the source of truth.** The LLM may only reword the body, and the compliance sentence is fixed.
- **Settings.** Gemini runs at temperature 0. A rewrite that fails any guard is discarded (`docs/LLM.md`).
- **Evaluation set.** 360 live suggestions, 60 per product.

**Limitations**

- **The Gemini sample is small** (12 rewrites), because a free-tier key allows about 20 requests a day per model. The
  360-item evaluation therefore uses the local Qwen2.5-1.5B: 89.7% pass.
- **The guards check facts, not every shade of meaning.** They check numbers, currency, time period, banned claims,
  the data date and length. A rewrite that kept every number but shifted emphasis would pass. Nobody has rated the
  explanations by hand.
- **LLM text is slightly harder to read** than the templates (reading ease
  67.3 against 73.1), so
  templates stay the default.
- **English only.**

| `attribution` | window_days 90, control_share 0.1, incremental_share_scenarios 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 | how a take-up is linked to a suggestion and how uplift is measured |

**Limitations**

- **The bank's economics are assumptions:** rates, default probabilities, losses, costs and margins. Every business
  figure is shown next to them.
- **The money a suggestion earns depends on the uplift it causes**, which has to be measured. The measured uplift
  (+0.7 points, 95% interval -5.7 to +7.1) is not
  distinguishable from zero. So value is reported as value at stake and break-even uplift, with scenarios, not as
  profit.
- **The backtest uses the test customers at the training snapshot.** Take-ups in the simulator are not caused by
  suggestions, so the backtest compares targeting, not persuasion.

## 11. Evaluation

**Assumptions**

- **Splits.** Customers are split 70/15/15 (seeded) into train, validation and test. Validation chooses settings;
  test is used only for the final numbers.
- **Baselines.** Rules and logistic regression for FR3, rules only for FR2, and random choice for targeting.
- **Oracle.** The simulator's exact probability is the upper bound (evaluation only).

**Limitations**

- **One synthetic world, one seed, one time step** (training snapshot to label window). Drift over time is not
  tested beyond the two snapshots.
- **Some of the brief's scenario tests are covered by unit tests, others by measured decision counts**
  (`docs/EVALUATION.md`, "Scenario tests").
- **No user study.** Explanation quality is measured by the guards and reading ease, not by customers.

## 12. Engineering and operations

**Assumptions**

- **Runs on one laptop** (14 GB RAM, 12 cores, a 6 GB GPU), with Python 3.11+ and internet for `pip` and the
  Gemini API.
- **`./run.sh` serves precomputed results** for 30,000 customers. `--quick` reruns the whole pipeline on 2,000
  customers; `--rebuild` reruns it on 30,000.
- **Batch, not real time.** Suggestions are recomputed per snapshot.

**Limitations**

- **The dashboards have no login and run as one local process.** Not production-hardened.
- **Nothing watches the system once it runs.** No drift monitoring, retraining schedule or alerting.
- **The free-tier Gemini key has quotas and may let the provider use prompts.** Production would use a contract with
  no data retention, or the local model (`docs/RESPONSIBLE_AI.md`).
